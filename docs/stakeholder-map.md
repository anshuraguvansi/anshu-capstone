# Stakeholder Map

The following are fictional design personas for the capstone. They represent the intended users of the platform and are not based on interviews with real students or residents.

### Maya Shah — Early-stage medical student

* **Role:** Second-year medical student who is beginning clinical skills training.
* **Current workflow:** Maya studies with question banks and written cases three or four evenings a week. These resources usually provide the relevant history up front, so she gets limited practice deciding what to ask. She sometimes practises with classmates, but arranging a suitable time is difficult.
* **Platform interaction pattern:** Maya would use the platform twice a week for a 15–20 minute practice session. She would work through a random case, ask about the patient's symptoms and history, and submit a diagnosis with a short explanation. She would look for feedback on useful questions she missed and details she did not include in her reasoning.
* **What changes if the platform works:** Maya can practise a patient interview without arranging a session with a classmate. She receives feedback immediately and can repeat the exercise with a different case while the previous attempt is still fresh in her mind.

### Rahul Menon — Final-year medical student

* **Role:** Final-year medical student preparing for clinical examinations.
* **Current workflow:** Rahul revises clinical cases independently and meets a study group once a week to practise patient interviews. The group sessions are useful, but the number of cases they can cover is limited and the feedback depends on whoever is playing the patient.
* **Platform interaction pattern:** Rahul would use the platform three or four times a week for short, timed sessions. He would ask focused follow-up questions, submit a diagnosis and supporting reasoning, and check the feedback against evidence from the source case. He would expect the synthetic patient to remain consistent and avoid revealing the diagnosis.
* **What changes if the platform works:** Rahul can practise between study-group sessions and cover more cases without needing another person to play the patient. The source-based feedback helps him identify missing questions and weak points in his reasoning before his next group session.

### Elena Garcia — Junior resident

* **Role:** First-year internal medicine resident with six months of clinical experience.
* **Current workflow:** Elena develops diagnostic experience through patient care, case discussions, and reading published case reports. Her exposure depends on the patients seen during a rotation, and reading a complete case report reveals the diagnosis before she has worked through the case herself.
* **Platform interaction pattern:** Elena would use the platform once a week for a 20–30 minute session, mainly for uncommon or complex cases. She would ask targeted questions, record a diagnosis and differential reasoning, and review the source evidence included in the feedback.
* **What changes if the platform works:** Elena can work through cases she may not encounter during a rotation without seeing the outcome in advance. Instead of reading the full report first, she can test her reasoning and then use the retrieved evidence to review the case. The platform remains a study exercise and does not replace clinical supervision or decision-making.
