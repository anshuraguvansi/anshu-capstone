Answer: The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI) is the regulatory authority for the securities market in India. Its main functions include:

1. **Regulating Stock Exchanges**: SEBI oversees the functioning of stock exchanges to ensure fair trading practices.

2. **Protecting Investors**: It works to safeguard the interests of investors by promoting transparency and preventing fraudulent activities.

3. **Regulating Market Intermediaries**: SEBI regulates entities like brokers, mutual funds, and investment advisors to ensure compliance with laws.

4. **Promoting Market Development**: It fosters the growth of the securities market through various initiatives and reforms.

5. **Enforcing Regulations**: SEBI has the authority to impose penalties and take action against violations of securities laws.

6. **Conducting Research and Analysis**: It conducts studies to improve market efficiency and investor awareness.

Overall, SEBI aims to promote a secure and efficient securities market in India.
